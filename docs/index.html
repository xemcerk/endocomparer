EndoComparer Comparative Surgical Video Question Answering with Pseudo-Siamese Visual Adaptation and Perceive-then-Compare Reasoning

Shi Li1,2,*, Lalith Sharan1,2, Joël L. Lavanchy3,4, Kun Yuan1,2, Lorenzo Arboit1,2, Nabani Banik1,2, Elif Yazici1,2, Maria Vannucci2,5, Vinkle Srivastav1,2, Pietro Mascagni1,2,6, Nicolas Padoy1,2

Abstract

Surgical assessment is comparative: surgeons judge a case against others, trainees against experts. Vision-language models, however, are trained and evaluated on single videos.

Comparing two surgical videos requires perceiving each video separately and binding every observation to the right video, and current VLMs often confuse which video shows what. We propose EndoComparer, a VLM for comparative surgical video question answering with two components. Pseudo-Siamese Visual LoRA gives each video slot its own low-rank adapter over a shared frozen vision encoder, separating the two videos in the visual pathway at the cost of a single encoder pass. Perceive-then-Compare reasoning trains the model to first state what each video shows, then derive the answer from these statements.

We also build EndoDiffVQA, a benchmark of 10,939 two-video multiple-choice questions over cholecystectomy and gastric bypass, covering anatomy, tool, action and skill, with every answer stated by the source clip captions or expert skill scores. EndoComparer outperforms strong generalist and surgical VLMs across all question categories. Ablations show that both components contribute, and the model answers more consistently when the two videos are swapped.

The task

Comparison is not single-video understanding done twice

Each item shows two clips of the same procedure and phase from different cases and asks whether they agree on one attribute and, if not, which video shows what. Every option opens with a verdict, then states what each video shows, so a model has to decide both.

Frames: four of the 64 uniformly sampled frames per phase-level clip used at evaluation. Distractors are named errors: swap puts the correct facts on the wrong videos, collapse flips the verdict, substitute keeps the direction but changes the magnitude, and absent (caption lane) names a value neither video shows.

Why it is hard. The model must perceive each video, keep the two sets of observations apart and bind every fact to the video it came from. In surgery the two videos show the same procedure, phase and instruments, so confusing which video shows what is both likely and hard to notice. Multiple-choice evaluation adds a trap: a model that always answers “the same” is rewarded whenever differences are rare.

EndoDiffVQA

A benchmark built from captions and expert skill scores

One formulation handles both kinds of annotation: a fact extractor reads a per-clip fact for one attribute, a deterministic rule turns the two facts into a relation between the videos, and a template renders the relation into a stem, four options and the correct letter.

Clips and annotations

Captions describe single-scene clips of seconds to tens of seconds (Anatomy, Tool, Action). Skill scores rate phase-level clips of tens of minutes with OSATS-style criteria (CholeScore) and GOALS domains (MultiBypass140).

Per-video facts

For captions, Qwen3.8-27B fills a schema with one slot per attribute, followed by synonym folding, value regularisation and removal of tool numbering. For skill, the expert score is the fact.

Pairing and relations

Split by case first. Two clips pair only if they share procedure and phase and come from different cases. The relation is one of same, different, only 1, only 2, neither; for skill, the sign of the score difference.

QA and audit

13 templates render each relation into a stem and four options. Keys whose candidates fall over 85 % into one answer cell are dropped, and the correct letter is uniform over a–d per split and template. Every recurring audit error became a hard rule each build must pass.

Metrics

Detecting a difference is not the same as specifying it

The five relations group into Same = {same, neither} and Different = {different, only 1, only 2}. Each source is scored with three metrics, reported as the mean over the four sources (4-source macro). Every answer weighs the same, so collapsing to one answer scores low whichever answer it is.

Detect macro-F1

Macro-F1 of the Same / Different decision. It charges both missed differences and false alarms.

Specify accuracy

Among items whose correct answer is Different and which the model also called Different, the share where it picks the exact option: which video, which value, how much better. A source where the model calls fewer than 20 items Different is excluded and marked (†).

Strict macro-F1

Macro-F1 over exact answers, averaged over the answer classes of each question family and then over families. For presence: {same, only 1, only 2, neither}; for skill: {better in 1, better in 2, same}.

Why not accuracy. A model that always answers “the same” is rewarded by accuracy whenever differences are rare, and models also exploit option position and wording. Strict weighs every exact answer equally within each question family, so collapsing to one answer scores low, and Detect and Specify show where the remaining errors come from.

EndoComparer

Keep the two videos apart where they can be confused

A Siamese vision encoder applies identical weights to both inputs and leaves the binding entirely to the language model. EndoComparer separates the two videos in the visual pathway and in the reasoning.

Pseudo-Siamese Visual LoRA

For every frozen linear layer W0 of the visual pathway (the QKV and output projections and the two MLP layers of each vision block, and the two merger layers), slot k ∈ {1, 2} gets its own low-rank pair: Wk = W0 + (α/ρ) BkAk. The two branches share the frozen backbone and the architecture but not the adaptation.

Token-level slot routing. Both videos are encoded in one packed sequence with a block-diagonal attention mask. Each adapted layer routes a token to adapter 1 or 2 by its position in the sequence, so a single forward pass computes exactly the two branches at the cost of one encoder pass.

Slot 2 is initialised from slot 1’s A with B = 0, so both branches start from the backbone. Because slot is defined by position and the order is swapped during training, every clip is seen by both branches.

Perceive-then-Compare CoT

A comparative answer is a function of two single-video facts. The per-video facts from dataset construction are rendered as short perception statements h1, h2 (a presence state, an instrument count, an attribute value or a score), and the target response chains them with the letter:

Video 1: h1. Video 2: h2. (y*)

The likelihood factorises as perceive × compare: p(h1 | x) · p(h2 | x, h1) · p(y* | x, h1, h2), so the letter is conditioned on the model’s own per-video statements, each grounded in the visual tokens of its own branch. Supervision comes from the same facts the benchmark is built from, at no extra annotation cost.

At inference the model decodes greedily and the prediction is read from the final parenthesised letter.

Training

Backbone Qwen3.5-9B. Vision, merger and language-model weights stay frozen; the two slot adapters (110 modules per slot) and a language-model LoRA (rank 32, α = 64, dropout 0.05, on the attention, MLP and linear-recurrent projections of all 32 layers) are trained together. Each training instance exchanges the two videos with probability ½ (mapping the answer through the swap map) and shuffles the options. Loss: negative log-likelihood of the response tokens only. AdamW, learning rate 10−4, cosine decay, 3 % warm-up, one epoch on train + val at an effective batch of 16 on two H200 GPUs, about 3.5 h.

Results

EndoDiffVQA is hard for current VLMs

All models receive the same input: 16 uniformly sampled frames per caption clip and 64 per skill clip, at most 784 × 448 pixels, from one shared frame cache, followed by the question, the four lettered options and the instruction. Decoding is greedy. Fine-tuned models are trained once on train + val for one epoch.

What the table shows

Zero-shot, open models reach only 30.6–37.2 Strict, and the surgical VLMs score below the general ones. Gemini 3.6 Flash is the strongest zero-shot model (46.9), close to the fine-tuned Qwen3.5-9B (48.3). Fine-tuning raises Strict by 13–19 points, but the gain comes from the caption subsets: on LRYGB-Skill the fine-tuned models stay at 23.6–31.9, no better than zero-shot.

EndoComparer is best on every column of its group except Specify. It improves over its fine-tuned backbone by 3.5 Detect, 4.4 Specify and 4.2 Strict, and over Gemini 3.6 Flash by 5.6 Strict, with the largest gains on the skill subsets (+8.6 and +7.8 Strict on Chole-Skill and LRYGB-Skill).

Ablation: the two components are complementary

Starting from the fine-tuned backbone (Siamese vision LoRA, letter-only target), PtC-CoT alone adds 1.2 Strict and PSVL alone adds 2.6, the latter almost entirely on the caption subsets. Together they add 4.2, and the gain on skill is larger than either gives alone: Chole-Skill rises from 44.9 and 43.3 to 51.9, LRYGB-Skill from 33.4 and 32.6 to 38.9. The per-video statements of PtC-CoT are most useful when each statement comes from its own visual branch.

Robustness to video order

The swap control re-evaluates the test items with Video 1 and Video 2 exchanged, scored against the swapped answer. A confused answer follows the position of a video rather than its content, so it is right in one order and wrong in the other, and the drop in Strict counts these answers.
